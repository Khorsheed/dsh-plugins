# @khorsheed/dsh-reader

The **inspiration space**: one **page-type tab** in the right sidebar that gathers RSS/Atom subscriptions and pasted article links onto a single wall. Clicking a card reads the body inside dsh (selectable, so it can be quoted); when the host's fetch cap truncated the body, the page says so in one line and offers a button to the original.

> The package name, tab kind and Remote namespace stay `reader` — they are stable identifiers (renaming them would change a deployed loader row and the on-disk state root). "Inspiration" is the name the reader SEES.

Fetching happens on the **host half** through the sanctioned `ctx.web` egress seam; the browser half only parses and renders. The subscription list and the most recent raw payloads are persisted to `$DSH_HOME/state/dsh-reader/state.json` with atomic `node:fs` writes — **not** through `ctx.fs`, which is the policy-fenced sandboxed filesystem (see Compatibility) — and a daily refresh is armed by a plugin-owned `setTimeout`, since no host scheduler service exists to ride.

## The three surfaces

| Surface | Contents |
| --- | --- |
| The wall | **Content first**: the entry cards own the first screen. One toolbar row reads **search field → filter → sort**: the field is the only flexible item and takes the leftover width, while the two icon buttons live in one fixed 26px box each to its right, so a narrow sidebar can never squeeze either of them out; every popover hangs under **the button that opened it** and cannot cover the other one. The filter is a **two-page popover** narrowing the wall four ways — read state (unread), by source, by KIND (a feed or a saved link) and by tag: the root page holds the read-state row, a by-source drill row that carries the **current value** (so no narrowing is ever hidden) and the three kind rows, and drilling in opens the source page, which owns the panel's height, its own scroll and — past six sources — its own search box, so **however many subscriptions accumulate, the panel's first page stays a fixed handful of rows**. It is the same local predicate as the search box (D14), so filtering costs no round trip, and its value is visible in the search box (`#sourceId` / `#rss` / `#link` / `@tagId`), which is also how it is cleared. **The wall translates too**: the globe at the right of the toolbar (its own switch, separate from the detail view's, sharing one translation memory). It decides **per FIELD**: a Chinese title or summary is left exactly as it is, a mixed card has only its English half translated, and Chinese is never translated into Chinese. Only cards that have **entered the viewport** are queued (IntersectionObserver feeds a queue, six cards per pass, grouped by detected language, through the article's own sentinel-batch contract), so scrolling back costs nothing and opening an entry does not re-translate its title. Two views: translation only (**hovering the translated TEXT** swaps just that FIELD back to its original — the title for the title, the summary for the summary; the card's blank space, tile and chevron change nothing) and side by side (each field's original stays under its translation and keeps that FIELD's own typography — a title's original is title-sized, told apart by ink and weight rather than shrunk to caption size). **The text boxes are fixed** — title and summary each reserve two clamped lines — so swapping languages on hover cannot resize a card or shift the grid; only the side-by-side view makes the grid taller, and that is a mode the reader picks. Entry cards are **cards** — a fill, a hairline border, a 12px radius — where hover only lifts the tone (never the geometry) and a keyboard focus draws a ring; unread entries ink their title and keep the **dot** (7px) on the tile's corner — session-only, never persisted — while read entries stay put and go quieter; the feed's own category and the reader's own tag share one pill metric (the reader's tag is outlined, and the row wraps), and tagging opens from the card's `+` into a panel where you tick the vocabulary or **type a name and press Enter to create it** (the native `datalist` is gone, replaced by stylable rows). A freshness line sits at the bottom — "Refreshed 3 h ago · daily 10:00" — so a reader can tell whether the snapshot is worth re-fetching |
| Detail | Where a card leads. The body renders as DOM text — **that is exactly what makes passage quoting work**, and why this surface never hosts an iframe. The body has **no reading measure**: it fills the width the sidebar is given, and paragraphs and lists share one right edge. Toolbar: back, **translate (globe + view menu)**, copy link, open the original in the browser, and — for a single saved link — **a delete button** (a feed entry gets none: the next refresh would bring it back, and a delete that undoes itself is a lie). Below the body, the other entries "also from this source". **When there is no body, this page treats two cases differently**: a KNOWN reason (bot wall / login wall / not a web page / cross-site hop / nothing to read) is stated and 「阅读原文」 is offered; a body that is merely **not fetched yet** (the feed gave a summary, the automatic backfill has not reached this entry, or its cache expired) is fetched RIGHT HERE on open — the line 「正在抓取正文…」 shows while it runs, the extracted text replaces it on success, and a failure falls back into the reason line above. **Opening an entry pays for one fetch**, which is what makes "open it and read it" true |
| Translation (on-device) | The toolbar's **globe** is a switch: lit means this body is showing a translation. Its caret holds three views — **translation only** (the default; a one-time line above the body teaches "click any sentence to see its original, click again to hide it"), **side by side** (every paragraph carries its original underneath, foldable per paragraph), and **original only**. The unit is a SENTENCE: clicking one sentence reveals that ONE sentence's original under its paragraph (hairline plus a highlight), and several can be open at once; when the sentence contains an inline link and was cut into several text runs, the reveal still shows the COMPLETE sentence rather than the fragments, the original line itself is NOT a control (it is text meant to be selected for quoting — clicking it does nothing, and a click that ends a selection is ignored, so quoting never folds the sentence away). The English original is set at BODY size (13.5px, the secondary ink, 1.62 leading, 3px between lines) and is spaced by WHERE its container sits: beside a paragraph it hugs that paragraph (4px above, 14px before the next block), while inside a list item, quote or table cell it sits 5px under the translation instead of being pulled up into it by a negative margin. An EXPANDED sentence keeps one quiet marker — a hairline underline, with no standing fill or bar (that is the "this sentence is bound to the line below" cue). The pairing highlight itself is HOVER-ONLY: pointing at a Chinese sentence lights that sentence (including the fragments a link split it into) together with its one original line, and pointing at the original line does the reverse. Nothing carries a standing colour or bar by default — the earlier always-on marks turned a skimmed article into confetti. All of it runs **in the browser** (the `Translator` API in Chrome 138+ / Edge 148+, desktop): the article never leaves the device, spends no tokens and writes nothing to `state.json`. Without that API, with an unavailable pair, or on a body that is already Chinese, the globe is **not rendered at all** |
| Add (dialog) | The header's plus, the empty-state card and the dashed card at the end of the wall all open the same **dialog** (the members tab's invite-dialog idiom: transparent overlay plus centered card, Esc and overlay-click close), because adding is a momentary act and the wall should not be replaced by it. **What comes back decides how it is stored** (D15): a feed becomes a subscription, a web page is saved as a single item. **A URL that cannot be read is still saved**: the verdict changed from "succeeded / failed" to "saved, plus whether it can be previewed", the reason is classified (bot wall / login wall / not a web page / cross-site hop / unreachable / HTTP error) and the card is marked 「仅链接」 — dropping the URL the reader pasted is worse than a card that can only be opened in a browser |
| Subscriptions (page) | The header's settings button opens the **subscription page**: two rows of chips at the top — **kind** (all / feeds / saved links, each with its count) and **order** (added / name / last fetch, added-time descending by default) — then one row per source where **both the name and the feed address are editable in place** (a changed address is a different document, so it refetches on save; clearing the name falls back to the feed's own title), plus its kind, item count, last fetch, failure reason (classified, not the seam's raw sentence), per-source refresh, pause/resume, remove, and the **daily refresh time setting**. Those questions are about SOURCES rather than about content, so they do not live on the wall |

**Click semantics (settled as option B)**: a card click opens the detail view. Most reading happens in dsh; when the body is too long or the reader wants the original, the detail view's own button goes to the browser. The trailing chevron is a cue that the card leads somewhere, not a control.

## Quoting

The body is an app-level selection, so `@khorsheed/dsh-quote`'s overlay menu **already covers it** — this package builds no selection menu of its own. The detail view also carries:

- **Quote**: the selection (the whole entry when there is none) plus a source label, merged into the current conversation draft through the official input machine (the live draft is read and merged, never overwritten, never sent);
- **Sent to side chat**: shown only when the composition really has a `sideChat` service (the capability handshake decides), hidden otherwise.

`reader` ships its own thin typert Remote (namespace `reader`) with the verbs `capabilities` / `listSources` / `addSource` / `updateSource` / `removeSource` / `refresh` / `getBodies` / `quoteToSideChat`. The host half **never parses** a feed — parsing lives in the browser half, and `getBodies` only hands the raw payload across.

## Install

```sh
dsh plugin --profile web add @khorsheed/dsh-reader
# uninstall:
dsh plugin --profile web remove @khorsheed/dsh-reader
```

Restart the host afterwards. Exactly one thing is written to disk: `$DSH_HOME/state/dsh-reader/state.json` (the subscription list plus the latest payloads). Uninstalling leaves that file behind — delete it for a clean removal.

## Compatibility

- npm release line (`@deepseek-ai/dsh@0.1.5-rc.1`): ✅ complete — the `sidebar.right.pane.tab` seat, the `ctx.web.fetch` egress seam, version-guarded `ctx.fs` writes; no `pluginInventory` needed (this package does no preset self-hide).
- Source line (deepseek-harness master): ✅ (verifiedHost: 0.1.5-rc.1)
- **Why state does not go through `ctx.fs`**: `ctx.fs` is this repo's **sandboxed** filesystem — every mutation is fenced by the FILE POLICY OF THE CALLING SESSION, so a workspace-write session is refused a write under `$DSH_HOME/state` (`FS_SANDBOX_DENIED`, observed on the acceptance instance). The fence is right and stays; the mistake was aiming a session-scoped capability at deployment-owned state. This package therefore writes with `node:fs` (atomic temp-file plus rename), the way this repo's other host-state packages do (`packages/lab/src/state.ts`), and degrades to memory-only when the root is unwritable.
- **Sources are named by the feed's own title**: the host half cannot parse a feed (it has no XML parser), so a new source starts with a URL-derived label — the `raw.githubusercontent.com/...` you see in the strip. Once the browser half has parsed `<channel><title>`, that real title replaces the display label, so cards read "Anthropic Research"; it falls back to the URL when parsing fails. The `{count} d ago` placeholder the list showed for an errored source was the other face of the same class of bug (an uninterpolated parameter) and is fixed.
- **Translation uses the browser's own on-device model, not the host's**: `self.Translator` (Chrome 138+ / Edge 148+, desktop only) translates inside the page's own process, so it needs **no host half, spends no tokens and needs no network** (the first use downloads a small language pack per pair, and `Translator.create()` requires user activation — the click on the globe is exactly that). The price is availability: Safari, Firefox and mobile have no such API, and the plugin then hides the globe while everything else keeps working. That trade is deliberate: sending one 50,000-character article through a host LLM costs tens of thousands of tokens, tens of seconds, and a second copy of the article to the model provider, while on-device translation is free, seconds and stays on the machine (its quality is page-translation-grade NMT, not LLM-grade).
- **The translation unit is a sentence — not a paragraph, not a text node**: sentences are cut BEFORE the request, so the pairing is true by construction rather than guessed afterwards — a paragraph-level translation comes back as one block, the model merges and splits sentences, and re-aligning the two sides afterwards can show the wrong original. Batches separate sentences with a sentinel and, when the returned part count does not match, that batch is re-sent one unit per request: **granularity is what the fallback gives up, never correctness**. A sentence that crosses an inline element (a link inside it) becomes two units and the link itself is never touched; nothing inside `<pre>`/`<code>` is translated.
- **A translator that cannot be built is reported, never faked**: `Translator.availability()` is a HINT, not a promise — on Chrome a pair has been observed answering "available" and then rejecting `create()` with `NotSupportedError` ("Unable to create translator for the given source and target language"; Chromium has the same failure recorded for macOS builds). So the plugin (1) asks the browser's `LanguageDetector` for the source language first — a wrong source is one way a pair cannot be built, and that error names NEITHER language; (2) actually tries the target in both accepted spellings, `zh` then `zh-Hans`; and (3) when every attempt is rejected as unsupported, HIDES the globe and says which pairs it tried while pointing at `chrome://on-device-internals` ("Broker State"). Non-permanent failures (download, quota) keep the globe so the reader can retry, and report the attempted pairs plus the reason.
- **Restoring the body is byte-exact**: translation only rewrites text nodes (the originals stay in memory), so "original only" and cancel put back exactly the HTML the host sent. That is also why a translation cannot lose links, images or tables — they are never touched.
- **Some sites refuse automatic fetches, and the button says so honestly**: measured, `openai.com` answers **403 to every non-browser request** (with a proxy, without one, and with a browser User-Agent — it returns a bot-challenge page). That is not an extraction failure and no retry fixes it, so for a 401/403 the detail view says "this publisher refuses automatic fetches" instead of "could not extract". **The status code is not the only signal**: an `openreview.net` PDF link was measured answering `302 → /challenge?redirect=…` and then a **200** challenge page — trusting the status code stores that page as the article, which is exactly how "I added it and there is no content" happened. So the decision is a combination: whether the final URL moved, whether the visible text is too short to be a page, and the words a bot or login page leaves behind; any hit means the payload is NOT stored as a body and the link is saved as 「仅链接」. Newsletter platforms and paywalls behave the same way. It is also why browser-rendered fetching is out of scope here: that needs the browser pane, which is still `planned`.
- **Some feeds publish full text for some posts and only a summary for others**: measured on the OpenAI alignment feed, whose 27 entries run from **155 characters** (a one-line summary) to **62,044** (full text). Nothing is truncated; that is how it is published. The detail view says 「这条订阅源只发布了摘要 —— 全文在原文页面上」 (this feed publishes only a summary) with the original-page link, instead of the misleading cap note.
- **The host hands over at most 100,000 characters per payload, so an oversized feed can only be read in part**: this is the egress seam's default (`web-fetch-http`'s `maxBodyChars`), NOT this package's body budget (256 KB per source, 2 MiB total). The acceptance instance's feed is 646,905 characters; the host supplies the first 100,000, so only the two entries falling inside that prefix are readable. The plugin **cannot** work around it: `ctx.web.fetch` accepts `{ url }` only — no Range, no custom headers, no pagination — and vendoring `fetch` to bypass it would bypass the host's egress policy, which is not an option. Reading such a feed whole needs **no host code change**: the official patch overlay adds config to that row (`--patch <file>`, or the machine-level `$DSH_HOME/cordis.patch.yml` which applies to every profile):

```yaml
- id: web-fetch-http
  config:
    maxBodyChars: 2000000   # default 100000
```

Verified with `dsh --profile web --patch <file> --dump-config` (the composed tree carries the value), and then applied on the acceptance instance: the same feed went from "100,000 characters, truncated, 2 entries" to "**646,905 characters, untruncated, 21 complete entries**". Note that this package's OWN per-source budget must not sit below the host's cap, or storing the payload re-cuts it — it is 2 MiB per source, 12 MiB total. The boundary that remains is ownership — a plugin patch mounts its OWN row only (the repo forbids one package mounting or editing another's row), so this override is a **deployment configuration** decision, and it raises the cap for **every web fetch on the machine**.
- **Full text is filled in automatically — there is no button to press**: after each load the host reports which entries still lack text (at most 8 per run; a failed entry is left alone for 6 hours), and the browser half fetches + extracts them into a 24-hour cache (configurable on the subscription page), **two requests at a time**. An entry being filled keeps showing the feed's own text, the footer reports "Filling in 3/8…", and a completed card carries a "full text" badge. **Do not want to wait for the slice? Open that entry**: if no body is held locally, opening it fetches once right there and says 「正在抓取正文…」 instead of waiting for the backfill's turn.
  - Why not fetch them all at once: that would crawl every article of every subscription. This takes a slice per refresh and moves forward.
  - **What arrives is what is shown**: a publisher refusing us (401/403), an unreachable host, and an unreadable page are three different sentences, and the body area always shows the feed's own content plus "read the original" — never a blank page.
- **A feed the host truncates is salvaged, not discarded**: the egress seam caps a payload by CHARACTER COUNT, and a cap that lands inside a tag makes the whole document malformed — which used to mean **no entries at all**. The parser now keeps every complete `<item>`/`<entry>` (re-wrapping each fragment with the document root's namespace declarations, without which a fragment carrying `content:encoded` is itself malformed), repairs the cut-off block best-effort and flags it **partial** so its arrived text still renders, and the wall shows an "incomplete" line with the original-page link. The shape is pinned by a committed fixture (`tests/fixtures/feed-truncated.xml`).
  Measured: the acceptance instance's 646 KB feed (of which the host hands over 100,000 characters) now yields **2 cards** — one complete 42 KB body and one partial — where before this fix it yielded **0**.
- **The default filter is "all", not "today"**: a feed's newest entry is often not from today (the acceptance instance's feed was 8 days old), and a default `today` filter made a freshly subscribed wall look empty — which reads as "the subscribe did not work". There is **no today filter at all** now: the four ways to narrow the wall (unread / by source / by kind / by tag) live in the filter popover, and the button's own pressed state plus the `#sourceId` / `#rss` / `#link` / `@tagId` query it writes into the search box keep "what is hidden" visible.
- **The body has no reading measure**: paragraphs and lists fill the width the sidebar is given — no `max-width` (the earlier paragraph-only cap left a ragged band of empty space on the right that read as a broken layout). Want a shorter line? Narrow the sidebar.
- **The host fetch cap is a known limit**: `ctx.web.fetch` truncates a decoded body at 100,000 characters and does so **silently**. Articles past that cannot be fully extracted (measured: an Anthropic research page ~266k, theverge.com ~899k, newyorker.com ~1.97M characters); a summary page's title, author and description still arrive. The detail view therefore shows 「受限篇幅，内容未完整呈现」 with a 「阅读原文」 button. RSS feeds are mostly safe (measured: HN 11KB, BBC 25KB, 阮一峰 69KB, Simon Willison 83KB — 83KB already sits against the cap).
- **Degrade matrix**: no `fs` → state is memory-only and lost on restart (the handshake reports `hasFs: false` rather than pretending persistence exists); no `web` → a pasted link is still saved, marked 「仅链接 (unreachable)」 with 「阅读原文」, and no exception is thrown and no URL is lost; no `sideChat` → the side-chat action is hidden; no `quote` → the selection overlay is simply absent, while the entry-level Quote button keeps working.
- **A cross-origin redirect is followed only when it can be reconstructed**: the host seam refuses one (`WEB_REDIRECT_BLOCKED`) and reports the target **origin only — never the path**. So this service re-enters the seam only for a same-HOST scheme/port change (`http://host/feed` → `https://host/feed`, path and query preserved) and otherwise reports that the address moves to another site instead of following it — fetching the bare origin would return a different page and file it under the reader's URL. The earlier text here claimed "up to 3 hops"; that follow path was dead code, because it looked for the text `WEB_REDIRECT_BLOCKED` in an error MESSAGE that never contains it (the host puts it in `error.code`), so every cross-origin redirect surfaced as the seam's raw sentence. It now reads the code, with a regression test.
- **Refusals become 「仅链接」 cards**: a fetched page with almost no readable text (under 200 visible characters after script/style are stripped) that also carries bot-wall or login-wall markers is never stored as an article — **a challenge page never becomes a card's body** — and `application/pdf` and friends are refused outright by the egress seam (`WEB_UNSUPPORTED_CONTENT_TYPE`: only html/text/json/xml). Those cards stay on the wall, take tags, copy their link and can be deleted; opening one shows the reason and 「阅读原文」.
- **A permanent refusal is not retried automatically**: bot walls, login walls, PDFs, HTTP errors and empty pages are recorded as non-retryable (`isRetryablePreviewFailure` lets only `unreachable` through), so the automatic backfill stops knocking; the subscription page's per-source Refresh still retries by hand and clears the record on success. That is what keeps the plugin from tapping a site that will always say no every six hours.
- **No conditional requests**: the host seam exposes no custom-request-header entry point, so there is no ETag/If-None-Match and every refresh is a full fetch.
- **Nothing behind a login wall**: there is no credential-injection surface.

## Known Limitations

- **Subscribe, do not crawl**: adding a subscription fetches the feed itself and never pre-fetches every entry's body (that would blow up storage and amount to running a crawler). Opening one entry pays for one fetch.
- **A PDF (or any non-web file) has no body**: the egress seam accepts only `text/html`, `text/*`, `application/json` and `application/xml`, so `application/pdf` is refused before extraction even starts — and extraction is DOM-based, which a PDF does not have. Such a link is a 「仅链接」 card plus 「阅读原文」; this plugin is not a PDF reader.
- **A cross-site hop is not followed**: the seam reports the target origin only, so the path cannot be reconstructed. Shorteners and institutional proxies are therefore reported as "this address moves to another site" rather than guessed at.
- **Read state and bodies are not persisted**: they are session state. Persisting a per-entry read cursor would need a second, write-heavy document, and "what is new since I last looked" is the whole value of this plugin.
- **XML parsing lives in the browser**: the host has no XML/DOM parser (`globalThis.DOMParser === false`), so feed parsing has to happen client-side.
- **No browser-pane dependency**: `proposals/active/2026-09-13-browser-pane.md` is still `planned`, and canvas CDP frames cannot host selection quoting.
